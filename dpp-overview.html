1 · What DPP Compass is

The EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR, Reg. 2024/1781) and the Batteries Regulation (Reg. 2023/1542) introduce the Digital Product Passport: a machine-readable record attached to a product that travels with it from production to end-of-life. By the late 2020s, almost every physical product placed on the EU market — from batteries and electronics to textiles, furniture and white goods — will need one.

For large multinationals this is a six-figure compliance project. For an SME exporting to Europe, or supplying parts and materials into someone else's EU-bound product, the same obligation lands without the budget or the data infrastructure.

DPP Compass is the workspace that closes that gap. It pulls together the four things every SME in this position needs to do:

  • Account for waste in mass and dollars, so the business case for change is visible.
  • Find recycled-input alternatives in the supply chain and quantify the saving.
  • Track products in the field via QR codes so end-of-life material can come home.
  • Generate the DPP record itself — every required field, with status and verification.
Compliance is the floor, not the ceiling. The same data the regulator asks for surfaces where your business is losing money. Most teams find recoverable margin in week one.

2 · The two halves of the platform

SurfaceUsed byWhat it does
Manufacturer dashboardYour teamWaste, supply chain, BOM/BOP, tracking, recovery, DPP compliance, redesign opportunities — across all products and all entity modes.

The two are tightly coupled. Every QR scan in the app lights up in the dashboard. Every take-back drop-off feeds the recovered-material ledger and the recycled-content claim on the next batch.

3 · Three entity modes

The dashboard works for three positions in the chain. Use the toggle in the top right of the dashboard to switch between them.

ModeWho it's forWhat changes
End-product manufacturerYou sell finished products to consumers (white goods, furniture, lighting, electronics, textiles).Full consumer-QR tracking, take-back network view, consumer-facing DPP.
Parts supplierYou make motors, PCBs, frames, sub-assemblies for OEMs that ship to the EU.Batch-level DPP, B2B offcut return, your DPP feeds your customer's DPP.
Material supplierYou make polymers, alloys, fibres, pellets, billets sold to parts suppliers.Per-batch DPP with chain-of-custody, recycled content and carbon footprint as the headline claim.
A material supplier's DPP is upstream of everything. If you sell rPET to a parts maker who sells to an end-product brand, your batch DPP is what lets the brand claim recycled content on the consumer's QR scan. Strong material DPPs are now a premium revenue line.

4 · Inside the dashboard

Overview

Four KPIs — waste in dollars, recycled-input savings, products in market, DPP readiness — plus a 12-month trend chart and a live QR-scan feed. The donut shows what share of your input is recycled.

Waste & Value

Production waste by material category and by line, in kilograms and USD. Each material row shows the current disposal path (landfill, energy recovery, re-melt, etc.) versus the best-available path — with the dollar amount recovered by switching. Hotspots flag anywhere your scrap rate beats the sector benchmark or your waste cost crosses a threshold.

Supply Chain

Every material in your active BOMs, listed with virgin and recycled price per kg, recycled-content availability, lead time, and DPP traceability. A negative saving means recycled currently carries a price premium — you may still want it for the DPP claim. The supplier shortlist below filters to DPP-ready partners only.

BOM & BOP

Multi-level Bill of Materials and the production sequence (Bill of Processes) for the selected product. Each component shows mass, cost, recycled %, and country of origin — the data the DPP record needs. Each process step shows energy use, scrap rate and embodied CO₂e.

Product Tracking

Where your products are in the world — based on consumer QR scans. The heat map shows regional density; the stacked bar splits the installed base by lifecycle stage (early / mid / late / awaiting EoL / recovered).

Disposal & Recovery

What's coming back from the consumer app to your take-back partners — by stream, mass, value, and whether it closes the loop. Below: the partner network shown in the consumer app, and the reward scheme.

DPP Compliance

The complete list of DPP fields required for the selected product group, with status (complete / partial / missing) and verifier. The card on the right is a preview of what a consumer will see on the QR scan. The SKU roster at the bottom is the project plan — every SKU you sell into the EU, and what is blocking its DPP.

Circular Opportunities

For the selected product, automatic flags across five lenses (disassembly, recyclability, life extension, material substitution, service model). Each opportunity comes with a per-unit savings estimate, a CO₂e estimate, and tags. The scorecard at the bottom is the product's circularity profile.

5 · How data flows through the platform

  1. You record the BOM and BOP per product — the master data.
  2. The platform computes waste, cost, recycled-content and footprint per SKU from those records.
  3. It checks them against DPP required fields for the product group and surfaces what is missing.
  4. It generates a QR code per unit, linked to the live DPP record.
  5. The consumer app exposes the DPP, repair instructions, and take-back locations.
  6. Consumer scans (purchase, mid-life, end-of-life) feed back into the tracking module.
  7. Take-back drop-offs feed the recovery module, which feeds the next batch's recycled content — closing the loop.
Note on the demo. All figures shown in the demo workspace are illustrative, populated to make the workflow legible. Production deployments connect to your existing MRP/ERP via API or CSV import.

6 · A 60-second primer on the EU DPP

WhatDetail
Legal basisESPR (EU 2024/1781) for most product groups; EU 2023/1542 for batteries.
First product groupsBatteries (Feb 2027), then textiles, electronics, ICT, furniture, iron & steel, tyres, detergents.
FormatMachine-readable record linked from a data carrier on the product — usually a QR code or NFC tag.
Who must complyAny economic operator placing the product on the EU market. Non-EU exporters comply via their EU authorised representative.
Typical contentIdentifier, manufacturer, material composition, recycled content, substances of concern, carbon footprint, repair info, expected lifetime, end-of-life instructions.
VerificationMandatory fields must be sourced and verifiable. Third-party verification is required for some product groups and recommended for all.

7 · A 4–6 month rollout

Most SMEs can close the gap to a market-ready DPP in 4–6 months of focused work if they start now. The recommended sequence:

  1. Pick the entity mode in the dashboard header that matches what you sell.
  2. Pick one product first. The highest EU-revenue product is usually the right choice — pilot it end-to-end before scaling across the catalogue.
  3. Open BOM & BOP. Make sure the structure matches your actual product. Every gap here becomes a DPP gap.
  4. Open DPP Compliance. The missing/partial rows are your concrete task list — one row, one task.
  5. Open Supply Chain. Identify two materials where recycled input is high-availability and either price-positive or at parity. Those are the quick wins.
  6. Open Circular Opportunities. Pick the top opportunity by combined dollar and CO₂ impact, and brief the design team.
